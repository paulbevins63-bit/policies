Personal Assistance

Food, shelter, and help near you — anywhere in the country

This is the part of the app for when you need something practical: a meal, a bed, clothes, help with a bill, a job. It works in all fifty states.

Always call before you go. Listings go out of date — food giveaways and meetings are often still listed months after they've ended. The app says this on every results screen, and it means it.

Opening it

Personal Assistance on the home screen.

The service buttons are dim until the app knows roughly where you are. That's the whole first job — section 3.

Not the same as the Churches button. The home screen's Churches button finds places to worship. Church Aid inside Personal Assistance finds churches that give practical help — food pantries, utility assistance, clothes. Different jobs, different lists.

2

The safety screen — read this one

The first time you open Personal Assistance, a safety screen appears, and again about once a month after that.

It is not boilerplate. Three promises, and the app keeps all three:

  • Shelter addresses are never saved. Not on your phone, not anywhere. For safe shelters you get a name and a number — they arrange where you go.
  • The app keeps no history. What you searched for and who you called are not recorded here. Tapping a number hands it to your normal phone app, so it appears in your call log as a bare number with no name on it.
  • The Weather button really shows the weather. It sits at the top right of every screen in this section, and it is exactly what it says it is.

Those promises are not the opposite of keeping you safe — they are how the app keeps you safe. An app somebody is afraid to have on their phone gets deleted, and a deleted app helps nobody. Private is what lets it stay in your pocket, and your pocket is where it works.

These exist because somebody may be looking at your phone. The app is built so that what it leaves behind cannot be used against you.

Tap Got it to go on.

5

Reading the results

Results are grouped, roughly: national and state programmes first, then local ones as the app finds them. Tap a phone number to call it; tap a website to open it.

The banner at the top is the important part: verify before you go. A wasted bus trip is a real cost when you're already short. One phone call first.

The first time you search a new area the app builds a local list, and it says so — "your phone may feel warm, this is normal." That's the scan working, and it only happens once per area.

6

County guides

Many counties have a published resource guide — one document listing local help. Where one exists for your county, a line appears above the location row naming it, with Download local resources beneath.

The app ships an index of over 2,000 counties across all fifty states and the territories, so there is a fair chance yours is in there. Downloading pulls the guide onto your phone so you can read it without signal.
