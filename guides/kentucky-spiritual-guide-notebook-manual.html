Your Notebook

The blank page, the doors, and what happens to what you write

This covers the Notebook — the part of the app where you write. Everything here lives on your phone. Nothing in this manual asks you to make an account or sign in to anything, because there is nothing to sign in to.

1

Opening it the first time

Tap Notebook on the home screen. The first time only, the app asks for three things.

All three are optional and you can change any of them later.

BoxWhat it's for
Preferred NameWhat the app and your companion call you. A first name, a nickname, or nothing at all.
LocationYour town, so the Recovery and Personal Assistance sections can find meetings and services near you. A town name is enough — it does not need your address.
Recovery Phone / Main ContactThe one number you'd want in front of you on a bad night. Your sponsor, usually.

Tap Save & Continue and you land on the blank page. You will not be asked again.

2

The blank page

This is the Notebook, and it is deliberately almost empty.

The design has one rule behind it: the Notebook always opens ready to write. Somebody may open this app only to jot down the phone number of the store they're standing in. That stays a one-tap thing forever. There is no "what kind of entry is this?" question to get past first.

What you can see:

  • Optional Title — leave it blank if you like. If you do, the app uses the first few words of what you wrote.
  • What's on my mind today? — the writing box. It grows downward as you type and pushes everything below it further down.
  • Choose your AI and the switch beside it — covered in the next section.
  • Submit Entry — saves what you wrote.

Below all that, once you've written a few things, your recent entries appear as cards. They sit below the writing box on purpose: what you wrote before is something you scroll back to, not something standing between you and the empty page.

The menu — every door

Everything else in the Notebook is behind the ☰ button in the top bar, between the back arrow and the home button.

DoorWhere it goes
NoteWhere you already are. The page is the blank note, so this just closes the menu.
StepsOpens up into three:
— ReadA Kentucky Path — the twelve steps written in plain language, to read.
— WorkStraight into the workbook: the twelve, each one opening the place where you write on it.
— Makin' It RightYour amends list, laid out one person per page to read out loud to your sponsor.
Recovery RoadThirteen places in the Bible where somebody stood where you're standing.
Personal InfoYour profile — section 6.
Choose Your AIPicks which companion you're talking to, if you have the MASH app installed.

Steps, Recovery Road and Makin' It Right have their own manuals. They're big enough to deserve them. This one stops at the door.

The switch beside Choose your AI on the main page is separate from the menu item. The menu item picks a companion; the switch decides whether what you write is shared with the one you picked. Off means off. If you have not picked anybody, the switch does nothing — there is nobody on the other end of it.

4

Writing something down

Type in the box. Add a title if you want one.

Tap Submit Entry. The entry is saved and appears as a card below.

That's the whole of it. There is no draft state, no sync, no "are you sure". It is written on your phone the moment you tap the button.

5

Reading it back

Tap any card to open what you wrote.

Every entry keeps the day and time it was written. Close puts it back. Delete destroys it — that one is permanent, and the app will not do it for you or on its own.

The app never destroys your writing. You always may. That's the rule the whole Notebook is built on. Nothing expires, nothing is cleaned up, nothing is tidied away after a while.

6

Personal Info

☰ → Personal Info. This is the longer version of the three questions you answered on day one, and it is all optional.

It's in sections:

  • Basics — name, location, phone.
  • Milestones — a clean date, if you keep one. Dates only, written YYYY-MM-DD. It exists so your companion can mark the day that matters without you having to bring it up.
  • How you like to be talked to — whether you want stories or facts, straight talk or gentleness, and what you're working toward.
  • Lines I won't cross and words that turn me off — things you want left alone. Worth filling in even if you skip the rest.

Tap Save Personal Info at the bottom when you're done.

Two more doors sit on this screen, below the boxes: Open my private entries, which is the only way in to the entries you marked private, and Back up or restore my notebook — section 7.

None of this is required to use the app. A man who fills in nothing but his first name gets the same Notebook as a man who fills in every box.

7

Where your writing lives

Plainly, because it matters:

  • On your phone. Your Notebook is stored on the phone itself, in an encrypted database. It is not uploaded, backed up to a server, or synced anywhere.
  • Nobody sees it because you wrote it. There is no account, no login and no operator with a copy.
  • Your companion sees only what you allow. Plain notes can go to the companion when the switch is on. Most of the Step work never can — the inventory questions about people you resent or have hurt, and your amends list, are refused by the app whatever the switch says.
  • Uninstalling the app deletes it. Android removes an app's private storage when the app goes. That is worth knowing before you uninstall to free up space — and it is what the backup below is for.

The backup, and the only way your writing leaves the phone

Nothing goes anywhere on its own. There is one way out, and it is yours: Back up or restore my notebook, on Personal Info. It writes a single file to a place you pick, and asks for three things — a password of at least eight characters, your name, and your date of birth. All three together are the key, so the same three answers open that file anywhere, on a new phone, years later.

The file is named to say nothing about itself: it is suggested to you as receipts-<date>.zip. It does not name this app or what is in it.

If you forget the password, the backup cannot be opened. There is no reset, and nobody can open it for you. Write it down somewhere safe. And keep the file somewhere other than this phone — a memory card, a computer, a cloud folder. A backup that only exists on the phone disappears with the phone.

After the first one it keeps itself up to date on its own, without asking again. Restoring shows you what is in the file first — how many entries, prayers, contacts — and writes nothing until you tap Replace everything.

The privacy is not a feature sitting beside the safety features — it is one of them. Nobody writes an honest Fourth Step into a notebook they believe somebody else can read.

8

If you write something heavy

The app reads what you type — on the phone, with a plain word list, not by sending it anywhere. If what you write looks like you might be in danger, a red card appears above your writing with 988 on it, and a text option, and 911.

It never stops you writing, and it never deletes what you wrote. The card appears beside your words, not instead of them. You can dismiss it with I'm okay and carry on. It is an offer, not a gate.

Your words are saved the moment that card goes up — before you've finished reading it — so backing out of the screen cannot lose them.

The card is not a substitute for a person. If tonight is bad, the number on it is answered by somebody, free, at any hour. So, usually, is your sponsor.
