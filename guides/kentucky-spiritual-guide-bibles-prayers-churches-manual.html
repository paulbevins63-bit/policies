Bibles, Prayers, Churches & Readings

The reading side of the app — most of it works with no signal at all

Four buttons on the home screen, and between them the largest part of what this app actually holds: fourteen complete Bibles you can read on a dead phone, prayers in both modern and historic wording, daily readings for a dozen traditions, and a way to find a church near you.

Prayers

Tap Prayers on the home screen. That opens the Prayers hub — everything is one tap from here, and there is no menu to work through first.

The app carries over 2,300 prayers, drawn from many traditions — Protestant, Catholic, Orthodox, and a great many that belong to all of them. Among them are the Prayer of Deliverance, St. Patrick's Breastplate, the Serenity Prayer in both its short and complete forms, the Jesus Prayer, the Suscipe, and a Daily Thanksgiving.

Three ways to find one

  • The search box at the top reads "Type a word, or what you want to pray for…" — and it means the second half. You can type fear, or my daughter won't speak to me, and it looks for prayers about that, not just titles containing the word.
  • Faiths, beside the box, narrows everything to your own tradition. Your own comes first; All is always there.
  • The shelves below — nine of them: The Daily Walk, My Personal Prayers, Nightly Reflection, People, When It's Hard, Recovery, Hurting, Before God, and The Church's Words. Each one opens on a list you read down.

Praying alone. The switch under the search box hides prayers written for a group — the ones that say we, us and our. If you are by yourself and those land wrong, turn it on and they step out of the way.

Some prayers carry two tabs. Where an old prayer has been put into present-day words, the plain reading is on the front tab and the original is verbatim behind it — same prayer, two voices, read whichever one reaches you. A prayer written plainly to begin with has just the one.

My Personal Prayers is your own shelf. The + on it offers to write one in your own words, and anything you put there, you can take back off.

Bibles — online and offline

Tap Bibles, at the centre of the cross.

Two kinds live here, and the difference is whether you need a signal.

WhatNeeds internet?
OfflineFour already installed — King James, Berean Literal, Catholic Public Domain, and Douay-Rheims. Ten more free to add. No. Ever.
OnlineNIV, ESV, NLT, RSV, NABRE, NJB, NAB and more, opened on BibleGateway.Yes

Why some are online only. Those translations are under copyright, so the app links out to the publishers rather than copying their text. The publishers keep what's theirs, and you still get to read it.

Other Bibles opens three more sections: ten more complete offline Bibles (ASV with Strong's, Berean Standard, Bishops', Coverdale, Darby, Basic English, World English, Geneva, KJV with Strong's, and the Literal Standard Version); partial ones, clearly marked — Old Testament only, New Testament only, or incomplete; and five further online versions.

Installing an offline Bible

Tap any Bible that isn't installed. A window says Not Installed, explains what's in it, and asks. Tap Yes, Install.

No internet is used. The text is already inside the app — installing just unpacks it onto your phone. The spinner is the phone working, not a download. When it's done the Bible gets a ✓ and moves to the top of the list.

Changed your mind? Not Now. Nothing happens.

Reading and jumping about

  • The book title is a button. It even says tap for chapters underneath. Tap it and every chapter number appears in a grid, already scrolled to where you are. Psalm 140 is one tap, not a hundred swipes.
  • ‹ and › beside the title step one chapter at a time.
  • Aa opens a text size slider, 12 to 32 point. Use it. There is no prize for squinting.
  • Bookmarks and History sit at the top of the Bible screen — what you saved, and where you've been.
5

Searching the Bible

Type into Search keywords… at the top of the Bibles screen and tap GO. Results come back as Book chapter:verse in gold with the verse beneath. Tap one to jump straight there.

Search works with no internet. It runs against the King James index already on your phone.

If you mention a reference like James 1:5 — or your companion does — the app can pull that verse straight from the offline text without a search at all.

6

Recovery Readings

Daily recovery meditations. Two doors, same list: Recovery Readings on the home screen, or Daily Readings inside Recovery.

It gathers the well-known ones — AA Daily Reflections, NA Just For Today and Spiritual Principle a Day, several Al-Anon readings, Hazelden meditations including Twenty-Four Hours a Day, Touchstones and Today's Gift — plus a small section of others.

7

Church Readings

Right arm of the cross. Daily lectionary and devotional readings, grouped by tradition.

Anglican, Baptist, Catholic, Christian in General, Episcopalian, Lutheran, Presbyterian and Orthodox. The Catholic section is the largest — Mass readings from the USCCB, homilies, Sacred Space and several devotionals.

Not the same as Recovery Readings. Church Readings follow the church calendar; Recovery Readings follow the program. Plenty of people read both.

8

Churches near you

Churches, above the cross-beam, finds places to worship near you.

Same four location buttons as elsewhere in the app — My Location, Zip Code, Select State, All USA Search — plus a Faith filter so you can narrow it to your own tradition or leave it on All Faiths.

Call before you go. Service times change, and a listing can be out of date. The number is right there.

This is not the Church Aid button. This one finds somewhere to worship. Church Aid, inside Personal Assistance, finds churches that give practical help — food, clothes, help with a bill. Different lists, different jobs.
