A Kentucky Spiritual Guide

Recovery

Groups, meetings, readings, and the people to call

This covers the Recovery section and everything it opens: the groups, meetings, readings, books, homes and local support, and your contacts. It is the largest part of the app and the part most of it was built for.

Two parts of it have their own guides: A Kentucky Path, the steps to read and the workbook to write in, and Recovery Road.

None of this is a program. It doesn't replace a sponsor, a meeting, a treatment centre, a doctor or a pastor. It is a place to read and a place to write.

3

A Kentucky Path and the Workbook

A Kentucky Path, subtitled From Your Porch to a Quieter Life, is the twelve steps, written plainly. The Recovery Workbook is where you write them: the Fourth Step inventory, Walk Me Through It, your amends list and Makin' It Right.

They have their own guide, A Kentucky Path, in the same list as this one.

4

Recovery Road

Thirteen places in the Bible where somebody stood where you might be standing, each with a page to write on.

It has its own guide, Recovery Road, in the same list as this one.

5

Recovery Contacts

Your call-first list: sponsor, home group, sober friends, crisis lines. Tap a number to call. Add these before you need them — the night you need this screen is not the night you want to be typing phone numbers into it.

Nearly every path through the recovery side of this app ends at a door back to this screen. That's deliberate: reading is not the last step, a person is.

6

What stays private

The recovery side holds the most private things you'll write. What the app does with them:

  • It stays on your phone, in an encrypted database. Nothing you write leaves it unless you send it — there is no account, no sync, and nowhere for it to go. The two ways it can leave are both your own doing: a backup file you make and save somewhere, and a PDF you print from Step 4. The app warns you plainly before either one.
  • Your companion cannot see any of it. Nothing you write in the steps or on Recovery Road, and nothing on your amends list, is ever given to the AI. The app refuses it itself, whatever your sharing switch says.
  • These screens can't be screenshotted or recorded, and show blank in the app switcher. That is on purpose: the person standing next to you is a likelier problem than a hacker.
  • The app never deletes your work, and nothing is cleaned up on its own. What you can delete depends on where it is. A journal entry has a Delete on it. Step writing comes off the phone only through the one-way clear-out the print screen offers you after it has made the PDF — nothing there is ticked unless you tick it. And the amends list can't be deleted at all; the A Kentucky Path guide says why.

If you write something that sounds like you might be in danger, a card appears with 988 on it. It never blocks you, never deletes what you wrote, and your words are saved the instant it appears.
