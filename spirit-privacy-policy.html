Privacy Policy — Kentucky Spiritual Guide

Last updated: 14 September 2026 · Kentucky Bootleggers Software Inc. · Contact: Paulie.n.SD@protonmail.com

The Kentucky Spiritual Guide ("the app") is a spiritual and recovery-support utility from Kentucky Bootleggers Software Inc. This policy explains what the app does and does not do with your information. The short version: the app was built to keep your information on your own phone.

What we collect and send to us: nothing, unless you email us

The app has no user accounts, no advertising, and no analytics or tracking of our own. We (the developer) never receive your personal information unless you choose to email us, for example to report a companion's answer (below). There is no server that belongs to us. We cannot see your journal, your profile, your prayers, your location, or anything else you enter. One Google component in the app does send Google usage reports; see "Scanning a page" below.

What stays on your phone

Everything you enter is stored locally on your device and is not transmitted to us:

  • Your journal entries and notes.
  • Your personal profile (preferred name, location text, phone, emergency contacts, recovery milestones such as a clean date or birthday, and the optional "how to talk with me" preferences).
  • Bookmarks, reading history, and any facts the on-device companion learns.

This data lives in the app's private storage. Uninstalling the app removes it.

When the app connects to the internet

The app is useful offline, but a few features reach the internet. In each case, only what is described below is sent, and it is never sent to us:

  • Opening readings and websites. Recovery and church reading rows open external websites (for example the U.S. Conference of Catholic Bishops, or recovery-literature sites) in an in-app browser. Those websites are operated by third parties under their own privacy policies; visiting them is ordinary web browsing.
  • Finding nearby help (Personal Assistance / Churches). If you tap My Location and allow location permission, the app sends your location to two places: to Google, through your phone's Google Play services, to look up the name of your town; and to OpenStreetMap's Overpass API, to list nearby churches, shelters, food, and support services. If you search Personal Assistance by ZIP code instead, the ZIP code goes to Google the same way, to find where it is. The app adds no name or account of its own to these lookups, and we never receive them; Google and OpenStreetMap handle them under their own privacy policies. The FindHelp and 211 links among the results open those services' own websites in your browser, and a FindHelp link carries the words you searched for and your ZIP code, if you gave one. You can decline the location permission and search by ZIP code or state instead.
  • County resource guides. Kentucky's resource list is built into the app and needs no connection. For any other state, tapping “Download local resources” fetches that one state's finished list (a few megabytes) from a public repository on GitHub. The request says which state's file is wanted and nothing else — no name, no account, no county, no search terms. The file is a plain list we prepared in advance; nothing about you is sent to build it, and it is cached on your phone so it is fetched once rather than every time. GitHub, like any website, can see the IP address the request came from.
  • Weather. The Weather button opens the National Weather Service's forecast for the town you have set in the app (or its home page, if you have not set one), in your browser. That is an ordinary weather lookup and carries no information about you beyond the town being asked about.
  • Scanning a page. "Scan a Page" reads the text of a flyer or listing you photograph with Google's ML Kit, on your phone. ML Kit also sends Google usage reports: information about your device and the app, performance measurements, and device identifiers, which Google uses for diagnostics and usage analytics. Those reports do not come to us, and the app has no setting that turns them off.
  • Reporting a companion answer. If you also install MASH, "Report this answer" under a companion's answer asks first, then opens your own email app with a message to us already written: the companion's answer, the companion's name, and the app and its version. It does not include your own words or any identifier, and nothing is sent unless you press Send. If you do, we receive that email from your email address, and a copy stays in your Sent folder.
  • The optional AI companion. If you also install the separate MASH app, the "Ask" feature shares the reading you're viewing and your profile with MASH on the same device so it can respond. That exchange happens locally between the two apps and does not leave your phone. The connection also runs the other way: if your companion understands that you need practical help — a hot meal, a shelter bed, a recovery meeting — it can ask this app for the matching listings near the location you have already set here, and show them to you inside MASH. Only that request and its answer pass between the apps, both stay on your phone, and nothing about your location is sent anywhere to do it. Domestic-violence services are excluded from everything that crosses: if MASH understands that you are trying to get somewhere safe, it opens this app's confidential-help screen directly rather than passing any data. Only apps signed by us can ask, and nothing else on your phone can.

Location

Location permission is optional, and the app uses it only when you tap My Location, to find nearby services. The town it finds is saved on your phone, and the Weather button uses the town you have set. The app does not track your location in the background and does not build a location history.

Addresses we deliberately do not carry

Domestic violence, sexual assault and trafficking services are listed by name, phone and town only. Their street addresses are removed before the data ever reaches your phone — they are not hidden in the app, they are not in the file it downloads, and they are not stored on your device. A confidential shelter's location is the thing that keeps the people inside it safe, so the safest place for it is nowhere in this app.

Web links on those screens warn you before opening, because your browser keeps its own history that this app cannot reach or clear.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them.
