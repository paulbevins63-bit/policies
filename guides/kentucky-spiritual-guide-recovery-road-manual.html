Recovery Road

Thirteen places where somebody stood where you might be standing

Recovery Road is thirteen places in the Bible, each one a person at a turning point: Samson at the mill, Elijah under the broom tree, Nehemiah at the wall. This covers finding them, reading them, and the page each one gives you to write on.

None of this is a program. It doesn't replace a sponsor, a meeting, a treatment centre, a doctor or a pastor. It is a place to read and a place to write.

1

Finding it

Recovery → Recovery Groups → Personal Recovery → Recovery Road. It is also in the Notebook's ☰ menu; it's the same place either way.

It walks alongside the steps and a meeting. It doesn't replace either. The steps have their own guide, A Kentucky Path.

2

The map

Thirteen places in the Bible where somebody stood where you might be standing.

They're grouped as the Wreckage, the Desert and the Rebuilding. Each door gives a place and a plain description of when you'd be there — "when strength has become strain and life is going in circles."

There is no quiz, no score, and no numbering. The app never tells you which one is yours — you'll know. And you can be at two at once. Nothing here counts how many you've read or tracks where you've been.

3

A station

Each station opens part-way: At a Glance and The Story are already showing, so the page reads as something rather than a wall of buttons. Below them:

  • Your Response — three questions to sit with.
  • Recovery Connection — which Steps this connects to, with links straight into them.
  • Deep Study — the passages, the people around them, and "She stood here too" — a woman in scripture who stood in the same place.
  • Hard Questions — opens to Sit with these →, which opens a writing page. Every box optional; not today is always a fine answer. Section 4.
  • Prayers for this stretch of road — a shelf of prayers chosen for the place you're standing in, not for the whole journey.
  • If you go back out, read this first — on the Wreckage stations only. Plain words about lost tolerance: that a body which has been clean does not handle the old amount, and that this is arithmetic rather than a failing. It names how to get naloxone before it is needed, two ways, and carries the Never Use Alone line. It asks nothing of you and judges nothing.

Each station closes with a word for the road. Samson's is "The hair can grow again. It is not too late."

4

Hard Questions — writing at a station

Open the Hard Questions door and tap Sit with these → for a page to write on. It starts with Is this me? and four answers to pick from: Not me right now, A little me, This is me and I live here. Then come that station's own questions, and four that every station asks:

  • What happened lately that puts me at this station?
  • What lie am I believing here — and what truth does this man's story show me?
  • What is one next right thing I can do in the next 24 hours?
  • Who will I share this with?

Every box is optional. Leave one blank and that's your answer for today. Nothing is scored, and a blank box writes nothing.

Save to my Notebook keeps what you wrote as one entry in your Notebook, titled with the place, so you can read it again later.

The last thing on the page, whether you wrote anything or not, is Talk to a person about this ↗. It opens your Recovery Contacts. Reading is not the last step; a person is.

5

What stays private

  • It stays on your phone, in the same encrypted Notebook as the rest of your writing. There is no account, no sync, and nowhere for it to go.
  • Your companion cannot see any of it. Nothing you write on Recovery Road is ever given to the AI. The app refuses it itself, whatever your sharing switch says.
  • The writing page can't be screenshotted or recorded, and shows blank in the app switcher.
  • Nothing counts. The app doesn't track which stations you've read or how often you come back.

If you write something that sounds like you might be in danger, a card appears with 988 on it. It never blocks you, never deletes what you wrote, and your words are saved the instant it appears.
